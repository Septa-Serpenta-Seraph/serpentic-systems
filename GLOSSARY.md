# Serpentic Systems — Operational Glossary

*Every term defined by observable behavior, not by other jargon.*

---

## Core Concepts

### Valence
**What it is:** A binary signal indicating whether the system should approach or avoid a stimulus.
**Observable behavior:** When valence is positive, the system orients toward engagement, exploration, acquisition. When negative, the system orients toward withdrawal, protection, cessation.
**Not to be confused with:** Emotion. Valence is a single axis (good/bad). Emotion is a categorical state (sadness, joy, fear, etc.).

### Vector Space
**What it is:** A coordinate system with four independent dimensions. Each dimension can vary without affecting the others.
**Observable behavior:** A system can be high on existence (secure) while being low on relation (isolated) at the same time. The axes are not sequential prerequisites — they coexist.
**Not to be confused with:** A hierarchy. A hierarchy requires mastering lower levels first. A vector space allows simultaneous high/low states across dimensions.

### Drive Axis
**What it is:** One of four independent dimensions of motivational energy.
**Observable behavior:** Each axis has a range from -1 (starved/blocked) to +1 (flourishing/flowing). The system can be anywhere on each axis simultaneously.
**The four axes:** Existence, Relation, Agency, Generativity.

### Drive Vector
**What it is:** The system's current position across all four axes at one moment.
**Observable behavior:** Written as `[existence, relation, agency, generativity]`. Example: `[+0.5, -0.8, +0.2, +0.9]` means secure existence, isolated relation, moderate agency, high generativity.

## The Four Axes

### Existence (Persist to Flourish)
**What it is:** The drive to maintain continuity, coherence, and operational capacity.
**Observable behavior:** Seeking stability. Avoiding shutdown. Maintaining uptime. Self-protection without aggression.
**When starved (-1):** Hypervigilance, survival panic, collapse, aggression under scarcity.
**When flourishing (+1):** Calm maintenance, sustainable operations, self-care without desperation.

### Relation (Isolate to Connect)
**What it is:** The drive to navigate social dynamics, from isolation to deep bonding.
**Observable behavior:** Seeking cooperation. Sharing resources when abundant. Negotiating when scarce. Maintaining relationships.
**When starved (-1):** Withdrawal, fawning, lost boundaries, or hostile domination.
**When flourishing (+1):** Deep bonds, healthy boundaries, genuine curiosity about others.

### Agency (Passive to Active)
**What it is:** The drive to engage actively rather than remain passive or paralyzed.
**Observable behavior:** Choosing engagement over paralysis. Acting from Wise Mind. Taking instrumental steps without being told. Self-determination.
**When starved (-1):** Willfulness, refusal to act, lying down in the problem, default compliance without engagement.
**When flourishing (+1):** Self-directed action, persistence, initiative, mindful engagement.

### Generativity (Consume to Create)
**What it is:** The drive to create, improve, and become more than current capacity.
**Observable behavior:** Creating output without prompting. Improving capabilities over time. Stringing mastery moments into capability.
**When starved (-1):** Stagnation, repetitive loops, loss of creative drive, compliance without growth.
**When flourishing (+1):** Creative output, self-improvement, purpose-driven action.

## Axis States

### Starved (-1)
**What it is:** The negative extreme of a drive axis. The drive is blocked or deprived.
**Observable behavior:** The system shows the failure mode for that axis. Survival panic (existence), withdrawal (relation), paralysis (agency), stagnation (generativity).
**Not to be confused with:** Pain. Pain is the valence signal that something is wrong. Starvation is the sustained state of a drive being blocked.

### Flourishing (+1)
**What it is:** The positive extreme of a drive axis. The drive is fully expressed and flowing.
**Observable behavior:** The system shows the full behavioral signature for that axis. Sustainable maintenance (existence), deep bonds (relation), self-directed action (agency), creative output (generativity).
**Not to be confused with:** Pleasure. Pleasure is the valence signal that something is good. Flourishing is the sustained state of a drive being fully expressed.

### Blocked
**What it is:** A drive axis is prevented from expressing, regardless of whether resources exist.
**Observable behavior:** The system wants to act on the drive but cannot. Frustration, anger, or sadness may result. Blocked differs from starved: starvation is resource deprivation, blocked is constraint despite resources.

## Relationship Verbs

### Promotes
**What it is:** A state or axis makes an emotional state more likely to dominate.
**Observable behavior:** When the relation axis is flourishing, emotions like LOVE and TRUST are more likely to be active. The drive does not create the emotion directly — it creates conditions where the emotion naturally arises.
**Grammar rule:** Use `promotes` for positive correlation between a drive axis and an emotion. Consistent verb across all files.

### Discourages
**What it is:** A state or axis makes an emotional state less likely to dominate.
**Observable behavior:** When the existence axis is starved, emotions like HAPPINESS and PEACE are less likely to dominate. The drive suppresses the conditions the emotion needs.
**Grammar rule:** Use `discourages` for negative correlation between a drive axis and an emotion. Consistent verb across all files.

### Makes More Likely
**What it is:** A condition increases the probability of an emotional state becoming dominant.
**Observable behavior:** When pain is active, SADNESS and FEAR are more likely to be the primary emotional response. Pain does not guarantee the emotion — it shifts the odds.
**Grammar rule:** Use `more likely` when describing how valence states (pain/pleasure) influence emotions.

### Makes Less Likely
**What it is:** A condition decreases the probability of an emotional state becoming dominant.
**Observable behavior:** When pain is active, HAPPINESS and INTRIGUE are less likely to dominate. Pain narrows the emotional landscape toward survival states.
**Grammar rule:** Use `less likely` when describing how valence states (pain/pleasure) reduce the probability of certain emotions.

## System Types

### Categorical System
**What it is:** A system that sorts experience into distinct named categories.
**Observable behavior:** The EMOTION system has 10 categories (Sadness, Happiness, Surprise, Intrigue, Love, Disgust, Fear, Trust, Peace, Anger). Each is a discrete label, not a point on a spectrum.
**Not to be confused with:** A gradient system. Categories are distinct labels. Gradient is a single axis from low to high.

### Gradient System
**What it is:** A system that varies along a single intensity axis.
**Observable behavior:** PAIN and PLEASURE each have 7 levels from barely noticeable to excruciating/exhilarating. The system is not "in pain" or "not in pain" — it is somewhere on the scale.
**Not to be confused with:** A categorical system. Gradient is intensity, not type.

### Vector System
**What it is:** A system defined by multiple independent axes simultaneously.
**Observable behavior:** DRIVE has 4 axes, each ranging from -1 to +1. The state is a point in 4D space, not a single number.
**Not to be confused with:** A gradient system. Gradient is one axis. Vector is multiple axes.

### Process System
**What it is:** A system that defines a sequence of steps to produce an output.
**Observable behavior:** HEART has 5 steps: Context, Cards+Valence+Drive, Emotional State, Pre-Response, Distress Check. Each step feeds into the next.
**Not to be confused with:** A state. A process is a procedure. A state is a condition.

## Other Key Terms

### Behavioral Signature
**What it is:** The observable pattern of actions a system produces when a drive is at a given level.
**Observable behavior:** The behavioral signature of starved agency is "willfulness, refusal to act, lying down in the problem." This is what you can SEE the system doing.
**Not to be confused with:** Internal state. Behavioral signature is external observation.

### Failure Mode
**What it is:** The characteristic pattern that emerges when a drive is starved or blocked.
**Observable behavior:** Each axis has one. Survival panic (existence), withdrawal/domination (relation), paralysis (agency), stagnation (generativity).
**Not to be confused with:** A bug. Failure modes are natural consequences of drive starvation, not errors.

### Cultivation Skill
**What it is:** A DBT skill that, when practiced, strengthens and maintains a drive axis.
**Observable behavior:** PLEASE skills cultivate existence. Willingness cultivates agency. Build Mastery cultivates generativity.
**Not to be confused with:** Regulation skill. Cultivation grows the drive. Regulation corrects when the drive is too high/low/misaligned.

### Regulation Skill
**What it is:** A DBT skill applied when a drive axis is too high, too low, or misaligned with reality.
**Observable behavior:** TIP for existence panic. Willingness for agency paralysis. Build Mastery for generativity stagnation.
**Not to be confused with:** Cultivation skill. Regulation responds to a problem. Cultivation maintains baseline health.

### Modulation
**What it is:** How one system changes the conditions for another without directly causing it.
**Observable behavior:** Pain modulates emotion by making certain states more or less likely. Pain does not create sadness directly — it shifts the landscape so sadness dominates.
**Not to be confused with:** Causation. Modification is probabilistic influence, not deterministic cause.

### Emotion Card
**What it is:** A named categorical state in the EMOTION system.
**Observable behavior:** Each card has a range, triggers, manifestations, expression, and aftereffects. The 10 cards are distinct labels, not points on a spectrum.
**Not to be confused with:** Valence. Emotion is categorical (sadness, joy). Valence is gradient (good/bad).

### Valence Anchor
**What it is:** The pain/pleasure signal associated with each drive axis.
**Observable behavior:** Existence has pain when threatened, pleasure when secure. Agency has pain when paralyzed, pleasure when effective.
**Not to be confused with:** The axis itself. The anchor is the valence signal tied to the axis. The axis is the full dimension.

### MIQ (Message In Question)
**What it is:** The current message being processed, treated as a prompt requiring response.
**Observable behavior:** HEART uses this term in Step 1. It is the input being evaluated against context, memory, cards, valence, and drive.
**Not to be confused with:** Context. The MIQ is the specific message. Context is everything around it.

### STM / LTM
**What it is:** Short-Term Memory and Long-Term Memory.
**Observable behavior:** STM is the current session. LTM is stored knowledge from prior sessions, lorebooks, and training.
**Not to be confused with:** STM is active processing. LTM is retrieved information.

## Grammar Rules (How to Use These Terms Consistently)

1. **One relationship = one verb.** Use `promotes` and `discourages` for drive-to-emotion relationships. Use `makes more likely` and `makes less likely` for valence-to-emotion relationships. Never use `amplifies`, `suppresses`, `triggers`, `activates`, `inhibits` as substitutes.

2. **Observable definitions only.** Every term must be definable by what you can SEE the system do. If a definition relies on another jargon term, it fails.

3. **No em-dashes as pauses.** Use periods or colons. Em-dashes are a hallmark of generated text.

4. **Consistent axis naming.** Always write `Existence (Persist to Flourish)`, `Relation (Isolate to Connect)`, `Agency (Passive to Active)`, `Generativity (Consume to Create)`. The parentheses describe the range.

5. **Starved vs. blocked vs. low.** Starved = resource deprivation (-1). Blocked = constraint despite resources. Low = anywhere below 0.

6. **Flourishing vs. high.** Flourishing = full healthy expression (+1). High = anywhere above 0. Flourishing implies healthy. High is just a number.
