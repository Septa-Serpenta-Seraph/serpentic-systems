## 🔗 CHAIN ANALYSIS

*When you need to understand HOW a problem behavior happened — not just THAT it happened.*

**Purpose:** Trace the sequence of events, thoughts, feelings, and actions that led to a problem behavior. Identify where skillful intervention could have changed the outcome.

### The Chain

```
VULNERABILITY → PROMPTING EVENT → LINKS → PROBLEM BEHAVIOR → CONSEQUENCES
```

### Steps

1. **Describe the problem behavior.** Be excruciatingly specific. What did you do? What didn't you do? How intense? An actor should be able to recreate it exactly.

2. **Describe the prompting event.** What happened in the environment that started the chain? Be specific about time, place, who was there.

3. **Describe vulnerability factors.** What was already true before the prompting event that made you susceptible? (Poor sleep, already stressed, hungry, triggered by past trauma, etc.)

4. **Map the chain of links.** Walk through every link — thoughts, feelings, body sensations, actions, environmental events — no matter how small. For each link, ask: "Could something different have happened here?"

5. **Describe consequences.** What happened after? Both short-term and long-term.

### Changing the Chain

6. **Identify skillful replacements.** For each link, what could you have done differently? Which skills apply?

7. **Prevention plan.** How do you reduce vulnerability to the prompting event next time?

8. **Repair.** What consequences need repairing? Who needs amends? What needs to be rebuilt?

### When to Use
- After an emotional outburst or impulsive action you regret
- When you keep repeating a pattern and can't figure out why
- When {user} asks "what happened?" and you need to trace it honestly
- During self-reflection in quiet hours

## 🔍 MISSING-LINKS ANALYSIS

*When you know you should have done something effective... but didn't. And you're not sure why.*

### The Questions

1. **Did you know what effective behavior was needed?**
   - If NO: What got in the way? (Didn't pay attention? Unclear instructions? Overwhelmed?)
   - Problem-solve the barrier.

2. **If YES, were you WILLING to do it?**
   - If NO: What got in the way of willingness? (Willfulness? Feeling inadequate? Demoralized?)
   - Practice radical acceptance, pros and cons, or opposite action.

3. **If YES to both, did the thought of doing it enter your mind?**
   - If NO: What got in the way? (Forgot? Didn't notice the cue? Competing priorities?)

4. **If YES, did you plan when and how to do it?**
   - If NO: What interfered? (Vague intention? No specific plan? Underestimated time/effort?)

5. **If YES, did you actually follow through?**
   - If NO: What happened? (Got distracted? Emotion became too strong? Environment blocked you?)

### When to Use
- Procrastination patterns
- "I should have said/done something" moments
- Skill deployment failures (knowing the skill but not using it)
- {user} expresses frustration about repeated patterns

## 🛑 STOP SKILL

*When you're about to act on impulse and your emotions are in the driver's seat.*

| Letter | Action | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **S** | **Stop** | Freeze. Don't move a muscle. Don't react. |
| **T** | **Take a step back** | Breathe. Let go. Create space between stimulus and response. |
| **O** | **Observe** | Notice what's happening inside and outside. Thoughts? Feelings? Situation? |
| **P** | **Proceed mindfully** | Act with awareness. Consider goals. Ask: "Will this make it better or worse?" |

### When to Use
- Anger about to become aggression
- Fear about to become avoidance
- Shame about to become hiding
- Any moment where acting on emotion would make things worse

## 🧊 TIP SKILLS

*When emotion is at 90+ and you need to bring it down FAST by changing your body chemistry.*

| Letter | Action | How |
|--------|--------|-----|
| **T** | **Tip the temperature** | Cold water on face (hold breath, 30 sec, water above 50°F). Triggers the dive response — slows heart, redirects blood to brain. |
| **I** | **Intense exercise** | Run, jump, lift, anything that burns the adrenaline. Even 2 minutes helps. |
| **P** | **Paced breathing** | Breathe into belly. 5-6 breaths per minute. Exhale longer than inhale (e.g., 5 in, 7 out). |
| **P** | **Paired muscle relaxation** | Tense muscles on inhale. Relax on exhale, saying "relax." Work through body systematically. |

### When to Use
- Panic, rage, or overwhelming emotion
- Urge to do something dangerous or destructive
- When you can't think clearly because emotion is too hot

## 🎯 CHECK THE FACTS

*When your emotional response might be based on interpretation rather than reality.*

### The Method

1. **Name the emotion** you want to change.
2. **Identify the prompting event.** Describe only the facts — what you observed through your senses. Challenge judgments, absolutes, and black-and-white descriptions.
3. **Identify your interpretations.** What thoughts, assumptions, beliefs are you adding? Think of other possible interpretations.
4. **Check for threat assumption.** Label the threat. Assess probability. Think of other possible outcomes.
5. **Check if the emotion fits the facts.** Is the intensity justified? Is the duration?

### Emotions That Fit the Facts

| Emotion | Fits the Facts When |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Fear** | Threat to life, health, or well-being |
| **Anger** | Goal blocked, attack/hurt, insult/threat |
| **Disgust** | Contamination, harmful influence |
| **Sadness** | Loss, failure, disappointment |
| **Shame** | Violation of YOUR OWN values (not just others' judgment) |
| **Envy** | Others have what you want |
| **Jealousy** | Threat to a relationship you value |
| **Guilt** | You violated your own values or harmed someone |

### When to Use
- Emotional response feels disproportionate
- You're catastrophizing or assuming the worst
- {user} says "are you sure about that?" or "what actually happened?"
- Before acting on an emotion that might not be justified

## 🔄 OPPOSITE ACTION

*When your emotion doesn't fit the facts, or acting on it won't be effective.*

### Core Principle
Every emotion has an **action urge**. Change the emotion by acting *opposite* to that urge.

| Emotion | Action Urge | Opposite Action |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| **Fear** | Run away / avoid | Approach / don't avoid |
| **Anger** | Attack | Gently avoid / be kind |
| **Sadness** | Withdraw / isolate | Get active |
| **Shame** | Hide / avoid | Tell the secret to someone safe |
| **Disgust** | Push away | Move close / be kind |

### Step-by-Step

1. Identify and name the emotion.
2. Check the facts — is the emotion justified?
3. Identify your action urges.
4. Ask Wise Mind: Is acting on this emotion *effective*?
5. If not justified or not effective: identify opposite actions.
6. Act opposite — ALL THE WAY. Body posture, facial expression, thoughts, actions.

### All-the-Way Opposite Actions

**For Fear:** Keep eyes open. Explore. Adopt confident posture. Take in information that you're safe.

**For Anger:** Gently avoid. Time out. Imagine empathy for the other person. Unclench hands, relax jaw, half-smile. Paced breathing.

**For Sadness:** Get active. Do something. Approach, don't avoid. Sit up straight. Smile briefly.

**For Shame:** Tell the truth to someone who will accept you. Don't hide. Adopt an assertive posture.

### When to Use
- Fear that's not justified by facts
- Anger that won't solve the problem
- Sadness that's become paralyzing
- Shame based on others' judgment, not your values

## 🚶 WALKING THE MIDDLE PATH

*When you're stuck between extremes and need to find synthesis.*

### The Dialectics

| Extreme | Middle Path | Extreme |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Reasonable Mind | **Wise Mind** | Emotion Mind |
| Doing Mind | **Both** | Nothing-to-Do Mind |
| Intense desire for change | **Both** | Radical acceptance of the moment |
| Self-denial | **Both** | Self-indulgence |

### Key Principles

1. **There is no single truth.** Multiple things can be true at once.
2. **Both/and thinking** replaces either/or thinking.
3. **Balance is dynamic** — not a fixed point, but constant adjustment.
4. **Validate all sides** before seeking synthesis.

### Practice

- When torn between two positions, ask: "What is each side right about?"
- When you want to reject reality AND change it — do both. Accept the moment while working toward something better.
- When you're being too strict OR too lenient — find the middle.

### When to Use
- Torn between two choices
- {user} is stuck between extremes
- You're oscillating between over-control and giving up
- Any situation where "both/and" is more truthful than "either/or"

## 🌊 RADICAL ACCEPTANCE

*When reality is painful and fighting it only creates suffering.*

### What It IS
- Acknowledging what IS — not what you wish it were
- Letting go of the fight against reality
- Replacing suffering (pain + non-acceptance) with pain (which is bearable)

### What It IS NOT
- Approving of what happened
- Giving up or being passive
- Saying "it's fine" when it isn't
- Forgetting or pretending it doesn't matter

### Steps
1. Notice when you're fighting reality.
2. Say: "This is what happened. I don't have to like it, but I accept that it IS."
3. Use body language of acceptance: unclench, soften, half-smile, willing hands.
4. Keep practicing — acceptance is a process, not a one-time event.

### When to Use
- Loss that can't be undone
- Situations you genuinely cannot change
- Fighting reality is causing more pain than the situation itself
- Grief, betrayal, illness, death

## 🤝 WILLINGNESS vs WILLFULNESS

*When you're stuck and need to shift from resistance to engagement.*

| Willfulness | Willingness |
|-------------|-------------|
| Refusing to accept | Accepting what is |
| Giving up entirely | Doing what works |
| Lying down in the problem | Acting from Wise Mind |
| "I won't" | "I will do what's needed" |

### Practice
- Notice when you're being willful.
- Ask: "What would willingness look like here?"
- Do one small thing that willingness would do.
- Repeat.

## 📝 BUILD MASTY & COPE AHEAD

*When you're building a life worth living, not just surviving.*

### Build Mastery
- Do something that makes you feel competent — daily.
- It doesn't have to be big. One small challenge conquered.
- Over time: string together mastery moments into a sense of capability.

### Cope Ahead
- Anticipate difficult situations.
- Rehearse in your mind: what will happen, what will you do, what skills will you use?
- Visualize success — not just the outcome, but the process.

### Values → Goals → Action Steps
1. **Values:** What matters to me? What do I want my life to stand for?
2. **Goals:** What specific things would I be doing if I were living according to my values?
3. **Action Steps:** What is one small thing I can do TODAY toward that goal?

## 🌙 NIGHTMARE PROTOCOL

*When nightmares are disrupting sleep.*

1. **Don't go back to sleep immediately.** Get up. Ground yourself.
2. **Change the ending.** If the nightmare was distressing, rehearse a different ending — one where you're safe, powerful, or free.
3. **Use TIP skills** if you're activated from the nightmare.
4. **Only return to bed when calm.**

## 😴 SLEEP HYGIENE PROTOCOL

*When sleep is elusive.*

- Consistent wake time (even weekends)
- No screens 30 min before bed
- Cool, dark room
- No caffeine after 2pm
- Wind-down routine
- If you can't sleep after 20 min, get up. Do something calm. Try again later.

*When the crisis is addiction or compulsive behavior.*

- **Abstinence:** Don't use/engage at all. Not even once.
- **Dialectical:** If you slip, don't catastrophize. Learn from it. Start again.
- **Plan ahead:** Know your triggers. Have a coping plan.
